Deaths at the Grand Canyon

Grand Canyon National Park draws millions of visitors a year — rim walkers, inner-canyon hikers, river runners, and people flying over it. Some of them don't come home, and the record of who died, and how, is scattered across park reports, newspapers, and old archives.

Deaths at the Grand Canyon brings that record together, from 1919 to the present: falls from the rim, heat illness on the trails, drownings on the Colorado River, flash floods, aircraft disasters, and deaths whose cause was never established.

Every entry cites its source. This is a research register, not an official census — older years mainly cover non-natural deaths, NPS records are anonymous, and some entries still need independent verification. See About for the full scope and limits.
